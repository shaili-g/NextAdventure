# Answer playbook

Reusable answer structures and habits for interviews. No personal claims here: facts about the user come from the master resume and `Profile/Story Bank.md`. When the user states a standing position in practice ("I always bias toward precision in regulated settings"), add it under "Your positions" so later preps reuse it.

## Answer structures
- Behavioral: Context → Approach → Validation → Outcome → Learning. Recap the problem first; end with what you'd do differently.
- Trade-off: Problem → Trade-off → Decision → Outcome, and always name what you gave up. Interviewers listen for the sacrifice, not the build.
- Reliability or risk: Failure mode → Detection → Mitigation → Escalation.
- End on the principle, not another caveat. Check in: "Should I go deeper on any part?"

## Landmines
- Don't claim you built what the team built. Say which decisions and trade-offs were yours and who owned the implementation.
- Lead with the stake ("we were losing a third of new users in week one"), not the title ("As senior PM at Acme...").
- Don't narrate process ("gathered stakeholders, drove alignment"). Say the decision and what it cost.
- Don't invent an opportunity cost. "Nothing was explicitly deprioritized; the team absorbed it, and that's the lesson" is stronger.
- "I decided" for your decisions, "we built" for team work. Never blur the line.
- Don't list credentials as floating bullets; use one number as evidence for the point just made.
- Don't teach interviewers their own specialty; ask instead.

## Recurring questions
- **First 30 / 90 days**: map how the product or system works end to end; baseline the real outcome metric (not a vanity proxy); find the few flows or customers that carry most of the value or risk; set up a lightweight way to measure change; run discovery in parallel. By day 90: what to double down on, what to stop, what blocks scale.
- **Ramping in a new domain**: "I'm not coming in as the domain expert; my job is to make the experts' judgment scale." Sit with the experts and users, study past failures, find the highest-volume and highest-risk cases, and turn that into requirements and measures. Pick one early call that doesn't need full domain fluency, usually measurement.
- **Why this level or this move** (e.g., an individual role after leading a team): say what the work needed then and what energizes you now. Never claim scope the master doesn't show.
- **Product-market fit for a B2B product**: production dependence (not pilots), customers building on top of it, unprompted renewal and expansion; contract cycles are slow, so instrument early.
- **What would you cut with six weeks and two engineers**: cut sophistication before trust; protect the core promise and the safeguards around it.

## Metrics menu
North star (engaged users, active accounts) · Acquisition (signups, lead conversion) · Activation (onboarding completion, time to first value) · Engagement (DAU/WAU/MAU, depth of use) · Retention (D1/D7/D30, churn, cohort curves) · Revenue (ARPU, LTV, expansion, margin) · Referral · Guardrails (latency, error rate, complaints, CSAT/NPS). For AI products add: true resolution vs. deflection, escalation rate, hallucination rate, reviewer override rate, calibration.
